# Win the Butler with receipts, not chat

The strongest entry to Exa's "Internet Butler" challenge is a narrowly targeted agent for **internationals living in Finland**. It answers "what changed for me since I arrived, and what must I do by when". It runs on the Exa SDK called directly from our own Claude tool loop, not through the hosted MCP server, and every claim carries the exact quoted passage it rests on. The Exa platform changed a great deal in 2026. **`/research` is retired, `/findSimilar` is deprecated, the search types are now `lite`/`auto`/`deep`/`deep-lite`, Exa Agent (`/agent/runs`) replaced Websets for list-building, and standalone Monitors (`/monitors`) handle "keep watching"** ([Changelog](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog.md)). Copying 2025 examples will break on day one, and an Exa engineer judging the demo will notice. Money is not the constraint. A typical 4-search run costs about **$0.028**, so $50 buys roughly 1,400 to 2,400 runs. The two ways to burn the credits are metered Agent runs (up to $5 each) and careless development loops ([Pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing)). The way to stand out is the fourth criterion, how clearly sources are shown. Independent audits found AI search wrong on more than 60% of news-citation queries ([CJR Tow Center](https://www.cjr.org/tow_center/we-compared-eight-ai-search-engines-theyre-all-bad-at-citing-news.php)). A butler that shows the before-and-after text of an official page, checked word for word against the fetched source, therefore makes a better case for Exa than the restaurant finders, rent checkers and chat UIs most teams will build. Two unknowns need answers from the Exa staff on Friday night: whether the hackathon credits unlock **Snapshot** (past page versions), and how well Exa indexes kela.fi, migri.fi and vero.fi.

## Exa's 2026 API surface has five pieces worth using

Everything goes through `https://api.exa.ai` with `Authorization: Bearer $EXA_API_KEY` or `x-api-key`. Both official SDKs, **exa-py 2.28.0 and exa-js 2.28.0 (released 6 Nov 2026)**, read `EXA_API_KEY` from the environment ([PyPI exa-py](https://pypi.org/project/exa-py/), [npm exa-js](https://www.npmjs.com/package/exa-js)). The OpenAPI spec (v2.0.0) is the source of truth ([exa-spec.yaml](https://exa.ai/docs/exa-spec.yaml)). The docs moved from docs.exa.ai to `exa.ai/docs/*.md`, with an index at [llms.txt](https://docs.exa.ai/llms.txt).

### Search: four recommended types, and outputSchema returns grounded JSON

`POST /search` is the workhorse. Exa now recommends **`lite` (~300 ms, cache only, $0.004), `auto` (~1 s, the default, $0.007), `deep-lite` (a consistent ~4 s, $0.012) and `deep` (4 to 15 s, $0.012)**. `fast`, `instant` and `deep-reasoning` are legacy, though still accepted. `neural` and `keyword` no longer appear in the spec ([Search quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/search/quickstart.md), [Pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing)). Independent benchmarks run in the week of the event agree: Exa fast had a p50 of 0.57 s and `auto` 1.40 s with a p90 of 1.91 s, the fastest of the providers tested ([websearchapi.space](https://websearchapi.space/blog/best-web-search-api)). The `lite` type has a trap. It **rejects with a 400 any parameter outside its short list, including `outputSchema`, `summary`, `subpages` and `maxAgeHours` (even `-1`)**. It also silently drops results that have no cached copy, and setting a parameter to `false` or `[]` counts as "set" ([Search quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/search/quickstart.md)). Never use `lite` for prices or availability.

The filters still in force are `includeDomains`/`excludeDomains` (up to 1,200 entries, with path prefixes and `*.` wildcards), `startPublishedDate`/`endPublishedDate`, `category` (`company`, `people`, `news`, `publication`, `personal site`, `financial report`), `userLocation` (`"FI"`), `moderation`, and `numResults` (1 to 100, no pagination). **Crawl-date filters are silently ignored, `includeText`/`excludeText` are deprecated, and `company`/`people` return a 400 if you add date filters** (`people` also rejects `excludeDomains`) ([exa-spec.yaml](https://exa.ai/docs/exa-spec.yaml)). The new `objective` parameter matters most for an agent. Exa recommends this verbatim as the tool-parameter description: "Goal for this search turn; say which documents should rank first, which should be excluded, and what specific facts or figures to pull from them." ([Best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices.md)). One behaviour is undocumented in most tutorials and matters for Finland: **Exa detects the query's language and returns results only in that language, and no documented opt-out exists** ([Changelog](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog.md)). An English query can hide Finnish-only pages. Issue both language variants, or pin `includeDomains` to the official sites' English sections.

Structured JSON comes back from `outputSchema` on any type except `lite`. Exa validates the schema before running, and the result is `output.content` plus **`output.grounding[]`: per-field citations `{field, citations:[{url,title}], confidence: low|medium|high}`**. The limits are hard. Object schemas allow **10 properties in total, counting nested ones, and 2 levels of nesting**, and every array must define `items`. Synthesis adds about 2 seconds ([exa-spec.yaml](https://exa.ai/docs/exa-spec.yaml)). Exa says "Do not add citation or confidence fields to your own schema; Exa returns them automatically." ([Deep Search](https://exa.ai/docs/search/deep-search.md)). It also warns against using `deep` "just because the output is JSON" ([Best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices.md)). Setting `stream: true` returns SSE only when `outputSchema` is set.

### Contents: freshness is now maxAgeHours, and subpages moved

On `/search`, content options nest under `contents: {...}`. On `/contents` they sit at the top level, and **a top-level `text` on `/search` is ignored with an `IGNORED_FIELD` warning** ([Best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices.md)). The views are `highlights` (recommended; Exa claims 500 characters of highlights match the first 8,000 characters of the page, [Highlights](https://exa.ai/docs/search/highlights.md)), `text` (markdown, `maxCharacters`, `verbosity`), `summary` (one LLM call per page; with `schema` it returns a JSON *string* to parse), and `extras` (links, images). Freshness uses **`maxAgeHours`: `0` means a live fetch within 60 seconds, `N` means a cached copy newer than N hours, and `-1` means cache only**. The deprecated `livecrawl` must never appear in the same request ([exa-spec.yaml](https://exa.ai/docs/exa-spec.yaml)). `livecrawlTimeout` defaults to 10 s; set 12 to 15 s for slow sites. Subpage crawling (`subpages`, `subpageTarget`) is **deprecated on `/search` and lives only on `/contents`** ([exa-spec.yaml](https://exa.ai/docs/exa-spec.yaml)). The `/contents` response includes `statuses[]` with `source: cached|crawled` and error tags such as `CRAWL_HTTP_403` and `SOURCE_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Log these, because the crawler respects robots.txt and does not get past logins or paywalls ([FAQs](https://exa.ai/docs/admin/faqs.md)).

### Snapshot: the most distinctive feature, and the least certain

Snapshot (launched 17 Sep 2026) pins Search or Contents to a past date. Pass `snapshotAsOf` (ISO date-time or `YYYY-MM-DD`) and Exa returns the newest stored version at or before that instant, with title, text and highlights all taken from that version ([Snapshot](https://exa.ai/docs/search/snapshot.md)). The deprecated alias is `crawledBeforeDate`. The constraints are severe. **Snapshot works with `auto`, `lite` and the legacy fast types but not with `deep`. It cannot be combined with `maxAgeHours`, `livecrawl` or `subpages`. The free tier returns `403 SNAPSHOT_NOT_ON_PLAN`. Pay-as-you-go accounts get a trial of only 100 requests, at most 1 URL per contents call** ([Snapshot](https://exa.ai/docs/search/snapshot.md), [Billing](https://exa.ai/docs/admin/billing.md)). The sources disagree on coverage. The API doc describes a rolling 5-month window, while the launch blog cites 400B+ archived pages over about 20 years ([exa.ai/blog/exa-snapshot](https://exa.ai/blog/exa-snapshot)). Plan for the stricter one: a demo comparing "before 8 Jan 2026" with today falls inside 5 months only for changes after mid-June 2026. The docs say nothing about how often versions are stored, so a version may not exist for a given day. The launch post lists RL leak prevention and finance backtesting as uses. **A consumer use, "what did this page say on the date I arrived", would be new to the Exa team.**

### Agent, Monitors and Websets, and which one replaced which

**Exa Agent** (`POST /agent/runs`, launched 16 Jun 2026) is "an async, usage-based web research agent API for high-compute tasks like list building, enrichment, and deep research". It returns `output.text`, `output.structured` (when given `outputSchema`, which has no 10-property cap), `output.grounding`, and `costDollars` ([Agent quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/agent/quickstart.md)). Effort sets the price: **`minimal` $0.012, `low` $0.025, `medium` $0.10, `high` $0.50, `xhigh` $1.00 per run, while `auto` (the API default) is metered up to $5 and `ultra` up to $20** ([Pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing)). It streams SSE events, including `agent_run.source.added`, which can drive a live "sources being read" panel. Run data stays retrievable for only 10 minutes after the run ends, and each run start counts as 2 requests against the rate limit. Exa Connect `dataSources` add partner data to a run, including **Jinko (flights and hotels with real-time pricing)**, Affiliate.com product catalogs, Polymarket, Similarweb and Fiber.ai, up to 5 per run ([llms.txt](https://docs.exa.ai/llms.txt)).

**Monitors** (`POST /monitors`; launched 30 Mar, v2 on 29 Oct 2026) are scheduled searches. A run retrieves results, judges them (dedup, relevance, novelty) and composes the output, then posts only new results to a webhook. Each run carries `output.results`, `output.content` (summary or `outputSchema` JSON) and `output.grounding`. It costs **$2 per 1k runs, down from $15** ([Monitors quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/monitors/quickstart.md), [Monitors v2](https://exa.ai/blog/monitors-v2)). The minimum interval is 1 hour (`1h|6h|1d|7d`), and a run may start up to 30 minutes late. The `search` block accepts only `query`, `numResults`, domain filters and `contents`. The webhook must be public HTTPS, not localhost, and is signed with `Exa-Signature` (HMAC-SHA256 with the one-time `webhookSecret`). For the demo, **`POST /monitors/{id}/trigger` fires a run on demand**, so nobody waits an hour. **Websets** still exists under `/websets/v0/`, with per-criterion `reasoning`, `satisfied` and `references` on every item. But Exa now tells new users "Starting a new list-building or enrichment workflow? Use Exa Agent", Websets needs a separate paid plan, and "small websets finish in minutes" ([Websets quickstart](https://exa.ai/docs/websets/quickstart.md)). It does not fit a live 60-second demo. `/answer` ($0.005, streamed, with per-answer citations but no per-claim spans) remains the fastest way to get a cited paragraph ([Answer reference](https://exa.ai/docs/reference/answer.md)).

Two smaller 2026 launches are worth knowing. **Exa Places** (5 Oct) adds 211M places with structured hours, operating status, phone and ratings on every endpoint at no extra cost ([Exa Places](https://exa.ai/blog/exa-places)). **Exa Search launched in the EU on 13 Nov 2026, the first day of Junction** ([Exa blog](https://exa.ai/blog)), which makes a Finnish use case timely.

## Build on the SDK; use MCP only to show it travels

The hosted MCP server is `https://mcp.exa.ai/mcp` (Streamable HTTP). It now exposes four tools: **`web_search_exa` and `web_fetch_exa` (on by default), `web_search_advanced_exa` (opt-in), and `agent_run` (on by default once authenticated)**. Nine older tools (`company_research_exa`, `linkedin_search_exa`, `deep_researcher_*`, `crawling_exa` and others) are deprecated and off by default ([Exa MCP](https://exa.ai/docs/get-started/exa-mcp.md), [toolRegistry.ts](https://github.com/exa-labs/exa-mcp-server/blob/main/src/toolRegistry.ts)). An explicit `?tools=a,b,c` list replaces the defaults, so list all four to be safe. Keyless access is rate-limited per IP. The changelog says 3 QPS and 150 calls a day; the source code defaults are 2 QPS and 50 calls ([Changelog](https://exa.ai/docs/changelog.md), [api/mcp.ts](https://github.com/exa-labs/exa-mcp-server/blob/main/api/mcp.ts)). Keyless access also needs a direct IPv4 connection. **A venue NAT shared by about 2,000 builders makes keyless use worthless, so always send a key.** If the MCP route is used, the `agent_run` tool says it defaults to `low` effort while the API defaults to metered `auto`, so pass `effort` explicitly ([agentRun.ts](https://github.com/exa-labs/exa-mcp-server/blob/main/src/tools/agentRun.ts)).

Exa's own documentation points application builders to the SDK. Its Anthropic tool-calling page ships `exa.anthropic.web_search()`, `get_contents()` and `handle_tool_use()` so you "don't have to hand-write the tool schema" ([Anthropic Tool Calling](https://exa.ai/docs/integrations/tool-calling/anthropic.md)). The MCP page positions the server for ChatGPT, Claude Code, Cursor and similar assistants. The SDK route matters for this challenge because the MCP `web_search_exa` hard-codes `type: auto` and highlights and exposes only `query` and `numResults` ([webSearch.ts](https://github.com/exa-labs/exa-mcp-server/blob/main/src/tools/webSearch.ts)). With the Anthropic Messages API MCP connector (beta header `mcp-client-2026-09-15`), Anthropic's servers make the call. We then see results only after the fact and cannot add retries, caching, `maxAgeHours`, domain pinning, or per-call `costDollars` ([Anthropic MCP connector](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/agents-and-tools/mcp-connector)). The trade-offs:

| Concern | Direct SDK in our tool loop | Hosted MCP via Anthropic connector |
|---|---|---|
| Parameters (`maxAgeHours`, `includeDomains`, `snapshotAsOf`, `outputSchema`) | Full control, fixed in code | Basic tool: none; advanced tool: the model chooses |
| Showing sources | Raw JSON (url, date, highlights, favicon) for our own reader pane | Pre-formatted tool-result text |
| Cost and observability | `costDollars`, `requestId`, `warnings`, `notices` per call | Not exposed |
| Resilience | Our own retries, timeouts, demo cache | No control |
| Setup speed | Short tool loop | Minutes |

The recommended hybrid: build the product on exa-py or exa-js. Mention in the pitch, or show in 10 seconds, that the same workflow runs in Claude Code via `claude plugin install exa@claude-plugins-official` or `claude mcp add --transport http exa "https://mcp.exa.ai/mcp?tools=web_search_exa,web_fetch_exa,web_search_advanced_exa,agent_run" --header "x-api-key: $EXA_API_KEY"` ([Exa MCP](https://exa.ai/docs/get-started/exa-mcp.md)). The Websets MCP has its own endpoint at `https://websetsmcp.exa.ai/mcp`. Its README warns that the unscoped npm package `websets-mcp-server` is not Exa's ([websets-mcp-server](https://github.com/exa-labs/websets-mcp-server)).

On the Claude side, one Anthropic constraint shapes the whole design: **Citations and structured outputs cannot be used in the same request; combining them returns a 400** ([Anthropic Citations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/citations)). Split the pipeline. Structured outputs handle intake and extraction. A separate citations-enabled call writes the deliverable from Exa results passed as `search_result` blocks inside a custom tool's `tool_result`, with one text block per highlight. That call returns `search_result_location` citations with the exact `cited_text` and block indices ([Search results](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/search-results)). Citations are then guaranteed to point at real text we hold, so the model cannot type a fabricated URL. Structured-output schemas compile a grammar on first use, cached for 24 hours, so **warm every schema before judging starts** ([Structured outputs](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/structured-outputs)). For the runtime model, the project's agent policy (Opus 5.5 minimum) covers Claude Code agents. The product's own API calls are a separate team decision. Opus 5.5 or Fable 5.2 is the safe default, and both support Citations, structured outputs and the MCP connector.

## Fifty dollars covers about 2,000 runs if the waste is avoided

Exa bills per request, with no subscription. Prices per call:

| Endpoint / mode | Price per call | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| `/search` `lite` | $0.004 | Cache only, restricted parameters |
| `/search` `auto` | $0.007 | Up to 10 results; +$1/1k per extra result |
| `/search` `deep-lite` / `deep` | $0.012 | 5 QPS limit |
| `/contents` | $0.001 per page per view | text + highlights = 2 views |
| Summaries | $0.001 per page | Extra LLM call on Exa's side |
| `/answer` | $0.005 | 10 QPS |
| Monitors | $0.002 per run | Webhook delivery |
| Agent `low` / `medium` / `high` | $0.025 / $0.10 / $0.50 | `auto` metered up to $5, `ultra` up to $20 |

Sources: [Pricing](https://exa.ai/pricing), [docs pricing](https://exa.ai/docs/admin/pricing.md). On these prices, a run of 3 to 5 `auto` searches with highlights costs **$0.021 to $0.035 (about 1,400 to 2,400 runs per $50)**. A single `deep` call with `outputSchema` costs $0.012. Even 200 judge queries at $0.10 come to $20, so most of the budget goes on development. The rate limits are tighter than the budget and **apply to the whole team across all keys**: 10 QPS for `/search` and `/answer`, **5 QPS for the deep types**, 100 QPS for `/contents`, and 5 QPS with 50 active runs for Agent ([Billing](https://exa.ai/docs/admin/billing.md)). Running out of credit returns `402`. A key that reaches its assigned budget also returns `402`, which lets each developer cap their own key. A 503 `SERVICE_OVERLOADED` is not billed, and retrying fixes it ([Error codes](https://exa.ai/docs/admin/error-codes.md)).

Exa's own guidance, ranked by payoff, comes down to a few habits ([Best practices](https://exa.ai/docs/search/best-practices.md)). Get search and highlights in one call (`contents: {highlights: true}`) rather than search followed by `/contents`. Request one content view, because each view is billed separately. Keep `numResults` around 5. Write queries that "describe the pages you want, including subject, source type, and time period", and pass `objective`. Use filters only for hard constraints. Avoid `maxAgeHours: 0` unless stale content would be wrong. Send `notices` to the model but keep `warnings` in the logs. A Python detail catches people out: **the SDK uses snake_case at every nesting level** (`contents={"max_age_hours": 24}`), and with no `contents` argument it fetches up to 10,000 characters of text by default. Pass `contents=False` when you want metadata only. Five things burn credits: Agent `auto`, `high` or `xhigh` inside a loop; summaries on every result; `numResults` of 25 or more with full text; retries that ignore `Retry-After`, which can trigger 429 cascades across the team; and the Snapshot trial spent during development, since it is capped at 100 requests. Cache every Exa response by a hash of the normalised request, both to save money and because the venue Wi-Fi will fail at some point.

## Pick internationals in Finland and show the page as it was

The challenge brief names rent checkers, benefits finders and restaurant planners, so many teams will build those. Exa's own demo gallery already includes **Exa Places, which answers "cozy restaurants near me for a date night" from a single `lite` call**, along with Shopping Search, Web Chatbot and Morning Briefs ([demos.exa.ai](https://demos.exa.ai), [Places demo](https://demos.exa.ai/places-demo)). A place finder, a chat UI, a company researcher or a news brief will look like a re-skin to Exa staff. The confirmed past Exa-prize winners share a pattern: a named person at a concrete moment of need, live facts no model could know, and an output you can act on. Examples are a tailored cover letter (PennApps 1st), a learning roadmap, and a wearable that researches the person you are about to meet (SuperAI 2026) ([Exa blog](https://exa.ai/blog/best-pennapps-projects-with-exa-search)).

Internationals in Finland fit that pattern better than anyone, and they are the people walking past the tables. **610,148 residents (10.8% of the population) speak a foreign language as their mother tongue** ([Statistics Finland](https://stat.fi/en/publication/cm1jg8tr20lco07vwvoif9s6i)), and there are about **37,000 foreign degree students** ([Kela](https://www.kela.fi/news/government-proposes-to-revoke-residence-permits-of-foreign-students-who-receive-social-assistance)). Their rules changed repeatedly within a year, which makes this the clearest case of information a model's training data cannot supply. Students lost general housing allowance after 31 Jul 2025 ([Kela](https://www.kela.fi/general-housing-allowance)). The residence requirement for permanent residence rose **from four to six years on 8 Jan 2026**, with no residence requirement for holders of a Finnish degree ([Migri](https://migri.fi/en/amendments-to-aliens-act-regarding-permanent-residence-permits-2026)). The home office deduction for wage income ended in 2026 ([Vero](https://www.vero.fi/en/About-us/newsroom/news/uutiset/2025/what-will-change-in-taxation-in-2026/)). Since 1 Apr 2026, a refused permit stops Kela benefits immediately, even during an appeal ([InfoFinland](https://infofinland.fi/w/kela-benefits-changed-in-spring-2026)). A **general social security benefit of €37.21 a day replaced the labour market subsidy on 1 May 2026** ([Kela](https://www.kela.fi/news/changes-to-kela-benefits-in-2026)). And a pending law, which would let a student's permit be withdrawn for receiving social assistance, was due to take effect around October or November 2026 if Parliament approved it ([Kela](https://www.kela.fi/news/government-proposes-to-revoke-residence-permits-of-foreign-students-who-receive-social-assistance)). Mistakes have exact costs. Without a tax card, the employer must withhold 60% ([Aalto formalities](https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2026-06/EU-citizen-Formalities-when-arriving-in-Finland_June_2026.pdf)). Secondary sites also contradict the official ones: third-party guides quote different work-permit income thresholds from Migri's ([Migri](https://migri.fi/en/working-in-finland/income-requirement)).

Each of these facts maps onto an Exa feature, which is what lets the Exa features carry the demo. Date-filtered `auto` search pinned to official domains finds the current rule. Snapshot shows the page text as it stood on the user's arrival date. `outputSchema` or Claude extraction turns amounts and deadlines into a dated action plan. A Monitor with metadata `{task_id}` watches the pending law and fires live from a "check now" button. In the table demo, a visitor types "non-EU master's student, Espoo, arrived Aug 2024, part-time job". The screen fills with a timeline of changes, each showing old and new quotes with dates, and ends in action cards: a drafted Kela message, `.ics` deadlines, and a link to the official form. The user approves each one; the butler sends nothing itself. That approval step keeps the product on the right side of the DoNotPay precedent, where the FTC fined a "robot lawyer" $193,000 for unsupported claims ([FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/86371)). Frame the output as "sourced information and a draft for you to send", never as advice.

Two alternatives are strong if Exa's coverage of Finnish government sites fails the first-hour test. The second choice is a **price-increase and terms-change fighter**: paste the notice, see the terms page as it was on your sign-up date (Snapshot), the termination right, and a drafted notice. Contract terms are versioned by date, for example Helen's "Terms of contract… from 1.4.2026" ([Helen](https://www.helen.fi/en/electricity/electricity-products-and-prices/contract-terms/terms-of-contract-for-easy-electricity-from-1-4)), which suits Snapshot. The third is an **EU261 claim builder** that checks the cause of a disruption against news from that day. About €3.2 billion of compensation goes unclaimed ([Resolver](https://news.resolver.co.uk/airlines-are-sitting-on-billions-in-unclaimed-flight-compensation-could-some-of-it-be-yours/)), but AirHelp and many other teams already work this space. The reform agreed on 15 Jun 2026 is expected to apply only from 2027 ([DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/agreement-reached-on-ec261-reform-to-strengthen-passenger-rights)).

### Show sources so a judge never has to click

Citation markers alone mislead. Experiments found that citations raise trust even when they are irrelevant or random ([arXiv 2504.06435](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.06435v1), [arXiv 2501.01303](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.01303v1.pdf)). The Stanford verifiability audit found **only 51.5% of sentences fully supported by their citations, and fluent answers were the less supported ones** ([Liu et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09848)). The display that wins this criterion makes the evidence visible without a click, as NotebookLM does, where "citations are direct quotes" shown on hover ([NotebookLM help](https://support.google.com/notebooklm/answer/14276569?hl=en)). The recommended design follows from that. Every factual sentence carries a chip coloured by verification state. Green means the quote was found verbatim in the fetched page text and a second-model entailment check passed; amber means a single source or a paraphrase; grey means "could not confirm", shown openly and never dropped. A right-hand reader pane renders the Exa page text and scrolls to the highlighted passage. Its header shows the source type (Official, News, Forum), published date, fetch date, and `cached` or `crawled` status taken from `statuses[]`. Snapshot items show the old and new quotes side by side as a diff. When sources conflict, both appear, with the official or newer one preferred and the reason stated. Exa's `output.grounding` confidence values and Websets' `reasoning` + `references` shape ([Get an item](https://exa.ai/docs/websets/api/websets/items/get-an-item.md)) give the "why we trust this row" model to copy. A cost tag per task ("this took $0.03 of web research", from `costDollars`) is a small touch Exa engineers will notice.

### What to avoid, and how to survive judges' own queries

Building on retired or deprecated pieces is the fastest way to lose an Exa judge: `/research`, `exa.research.*` (exa-js rejects locally with 410), `findSimilar`, `livecrawl`, `useAutoprompt`, `includeText`, `numSentences`, the deprecated MCP tools, and the legacy `fast`/`instant`/`deep-reasoning` types. A free-roaming agent is a mistake when the task shape is known. Anthropic's guidance favours prompt chaining with programmatic gates and parallel steps, while "prioritiz[ing] transparency by explicitly showing the agent's planning steps" ([Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). Use a fixed pipeline: intake (structured), plan (3 to 6 questions, templated per case type), parallel `auto` fan-out with highlights under a concurrency cap of about 8, full text for the top 3 to 5 pages only, extraction, verbatim and entailment checks, a citations-enabled compose call, then artefacts. Set hard caps of 12 searches, 5 fetches and about 30 seconds overall, after which the agent composes with what it has. Stream every stage to the screen ("Reading migri.fi…", "3 of 4 facts verified") so that latency looks like work. For queries the agent cannot answer, widen the filters, rephrase once, then abstain with a list of what was searched. Keep a replay mode for rehearsed cases, labelled honestly, plus a phone hotspot. Do not depend on Monitor webhooks reaching localhost; deploy the receiver or poll `GET /monitors/{id}/runs`.

## Conclusion

The judging criteria favour one behaviour more than any single feature: **proving something changed, from the page itself**. The Snapshot diff, the Monitor that fires on demand, and per-field grounding all serve that one demonstration, and each answers Exa's first criterion directly, because no model trained before mid-2026 knows Finland's six-year permanent-residence rule. The same behaviour serves the main-prize table, where a passer-by with a Finnish residence permit can type their own arrival date and see their own rules change on screen in under a minute.

The plan has two real risks, and both can be checked by Saturday morning. If hackathon credits do not unlock Snapshot, or Exa barely indexes kela.fi and migri.fi, the internationals idea loses its headline feature. The fallback then is the terms-change fighter, or before-and-after evidence built from dated official news pages rather than archived versions. Ask the Exa staff about Snapshot access, raised QPS and Websets plans on Friday night. Spend the first build hour on ten real official-domain queries in English and Finnish. Choose the final group and task only after those results are in.
