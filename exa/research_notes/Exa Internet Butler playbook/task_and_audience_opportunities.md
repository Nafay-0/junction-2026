# Task and audience opportunities for Exa's "Internet Butler" (as of 13 Nov 2026)

Scope: which specific group of real people plus which tedious task gives the strongest entry, judged on Exa's criteria (information not in training data, usefulness to a named group, shown sources, originality, live demo on judges' own queries) and the main-prize table criteria (Innovation, Impact, Implementation; "a real user problem", "something people want to come back to").

Method note: about 20 searches and fetches, run on 13 Nov 2026. Official pages (kela.fi, migri.fi, vero.fi, infofinland.fi, the Council of the EU) were preferred. Some figures come only from claims firms or third-party guides; those are flagged. Anything not found is listed under Gaps rather than estimated.

## 1. Who has the problem, how many of them, and what it costs them (evidence per candidate)

### Takeaway
The best-documented group for a Finland table is internationals living in Finland. About 610,000 people (10.8% of the population) speak a foreign language as their mother tongue, and about 37,000 are foreign full-degree students. Their rules changed several times between 2025 and 2026. Flight compensation has the biggest money figure (about €3 billion unclaimed in the EU, per claims-industry estimates). Consumer detriment is large too (€20 to 58 billion a year across six markets in a 2017 Commission study), but it is spread thinly over many cases.

### Cited Findings
**Internationals in Finland (candidates 1, 5, 10)**
- At the end of 2024, 610,148 people in Finland spoke a foreign language as their native language, 10.8% of the population (corrected figure) — [Statistics Finland, Population structure 2024](https://stat.fi/en/publication/cm1jg8tr20lco07vwvoif9s6i); [correction notice](https://stat.fi/en/revisionrelease/cm92crqci6ujc07w2pjqjer4s)
- A Kela-register-based release puts the foreign-language population at 373,000 in 2017 and 646,000 in 2025, of whom 506,000 are adults. Its basis may differ from Statistics Finland's — [Tietotarjotin / Kela release](https://tietotarjotin.fi/en/news/1435987/fewer-foreign-language-recipients-of-social-assistance-and-unemployment-benefits-than-before)
- Foreign full-degree students in 2025: 37,287 (Vipunen data via the ASEM compendium). About 15,000 international students were offered degree places for autumn 2026 — [ASEM compendium, Finland](https://asem-education.org/compendiums/finland/); [Study in Finland, 29 May 2026](https://www.studyinfinland.fi/news-events/welcome-new-students-2026)
- Kela's own figure for monitored third-country students matches: "333 of more than 37,000 students received social assistance" in a little over a year of Migri monitoring — [Kela news, 5 May 2026](https://www.kela.fi/news/government-proposes-to-revoke-residence-permits-of-foreign-students-who-receive-social-assistance)
- Universities reissue their arrival-formalities checklists several times a year. Aalto has separate EU, Nordic and non-EU PDFs dated Oct 2024, Feb 2026, June 2026 and July 2026. That shows both the churn and how complex the process is per nationality — [Aalto EU-citizen formalities, July 2026](https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2026-08/EU-citizen-formalities-when-arriving-in-Finland-07_2026.pdf); [Feb 2026 version](https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2026-03/EU-citizen-Formalities-when-arriving-in-Finland_Feb_2026.pdf)
- Cost of getting the sequence wrong: without a tax card, "the employer is obligated to withhold 60% tax". Banks need a Finnish personal identity code, DVV registration and a registered Finnish address. Strong e-ID (bank codes) comes only after the police ID card, which in turn needs a residence permit and DVV registration — [Aalto/university guides via search](https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2026-06/EU-citizen-Formalities-when-arriving-in-Finland_June_2026.pdf); [SAMK, Bank issues for non-EU students (Sept 2025)](https://www.samk.fi/app/uploads/sites/2/2025/09/Bank-issues-for-non-Eu-Students-1.pdf); [Univ. of Vaasa, administrative essentials](https://www.uwasa.fi/en/open-positions/expat/administrative-essentials-what-you-need-settle)

**Flight disruption (candidate 2)**
- About €3.2 billion in unpaid EU delay compensation is still claimable (June 2026 analysis reported via the FT). A claims firm (Skycop) reports that about two-thirds of eligible passengers never claim. These are industry estimates, not regulator figures — [Resolver news](https://news.resolver.co.uk/airlines-are-sitting-on-billions-in-unclaimed-flight-compensation-could-some-of-it-be-yours/); [Travel Weekly, ~70% unclaimed](https://travelweekly.co.uk/articles/62020/flight-delay-compensation-unclaimed-by-70-of-passengers)
- Older estimates disagree (AirHelp €5 billion; Skycop €2.2 billion). The methods differ — [BottOnline summary](https://www.bottonline.co.uk/blog/europes-airlines-are-sitting-on-billions-in-unclaimed-flight-compensation-are-you-owed-money)

**Consumer problems (candidates 3, 6)**
- Across six markets, EU28 consumers lost €20.3 to 58.4 billion in 12 months — [European Commission, Study on measuring consumer detriment (2017)](https://commission.europa.eu/publications/study-measuring-consumer-detriment-european-union_en)
- Harmful online commercial practices cost EU consumers at least €7.9 billion a year (Digital Fairness Fitness Check, Oct 2024) — [BEUC](https://www.beuc.eu/news/european-commission-reveals-blind-spots-protecting-consumers-online); [Lewis Silkin summary](https://www.lewissilkin.com/insights/2024/10/08/european-commission-publishes-long-awaited-digital-fitness-check-102jl80)
- Finnish electricity: the exchange price averaged €117/MWh in January 2026, the highest monthly average since 2022, and about 10% of consumers had recently switched to fixed contracts — [EADaily, 4 Feb 2026](https://eadaily.com/en/ampnews/2026/02/04/finns-will-scatter-fixed-contracts-electricity-has-doubled-in-price) (secondary outlet). KKV's 2025 retail market study says choosing the right electricity contract "has become more important" — [KKV research report 2025](https://www.kkv.fi/uploads/sites/2/2025-05-tutkimusraportteja-sahkon-vahittaismarkkinaselvitys.pdf)
- Retailers' contract terms change on fixed dates. For example, Helen publishes "Terms of contract for Easy Electricity from 1.4.2026" — [Helen](https://www.helen.fi/en/electricity/electricity-products-and-prices/contract-terms/terms-of-contract-for-easy-electricity-from-1-4)

**Benefits non-take-up (candidate 1, general)**
- Eurofound documents non-take-up across member states and names lack of information, costly or complex access, and stigma as the main causes. No single EU-wide percentage was found — [Eurofound, Access to social benefits: reducing non-take-up](https://www.eurofound.europa.eu/de/publications/all/access-social-benefits-reducing-non-take); the quantitative country comparison (DE, BE, UK and others) is in [DREES 2022](https://drees.solidarites-sante.gouv.fr/sites/default/files/2022-07/Non-take-up%20of%20minimum%20social%20benefits%20quantification%20in%20Europe.%20A%20salient%20phenomenon%20still%20not%20making%20public%20policy%20headlines_0.pdf)

**Rent (candidate 4)**
- Finland has no state rent control for privately financed housing. Increases must follow the lease's adjustment clause (usually index-linked, with an annual review date) or be negotiated. Either party can ask a court to review unreasonable rent under the Act on Residential Leases (AHVL) §29 — [Landager, Finland rent increases](https://landager.com/en/property-compliance/finland/national/rent-increases) (secondary; not checked against Finlex)

### Inferences
- In the Junction hall, the "internationals in Finland" group overlaps heavily with the people who will score us: students and young developers, many of them foreigners living in Finland. That makes the one-minute table test personal, which no other group offers.
- Flight compensation has the clearest money-per-case figure (€250 to €600 under current EU261), but the person must have had a disrupted flight in the last few years. Many attendees flew in, so that is plausible but not guaranteed.

### Gaps
- No Finnish figure for unclaimed Kela benefits or unclaimed tax deductions was found. No forum statistics (r/Finland) were retrieved: the search returned only institutional pages.
- No official figure for how many internationals lose money or time to registration-sequence mistakes.
- No quantified "overpayment" on Finnish electricity contracts was found (Energiavirasto data not retrieved).

## 2. Where the authoritative information lives, how often it changes, and why a model's training data would be wrong

### Takeaway
Finland's rules for internationals changed repeatedly between August 2025 and November 2026, and some changes are still pending in Parliament. A model trained before mid-2026 will give wrong answers on housing allowance, permanent residence, unemployment benefit, social assistance, tax deductions and EU261. That makes these rules close to ideal for Exa's first criterion, especially past page versions ("what did this page say before") and Monitors (pending laws).

### Cited Findings
**Kela (kela.fi, infofinland.fi)**
- General housing allowance stopped being paid to students after 31 July 2025. Students moved to the student housing supplement, except students living with their own or their partner's child — [Haaga-Helia, Kela changes to student housing benefits](https://www.haaga-helia.fi/en/current/news/kela-changes-student-housing-benefits); [Kela, General housing allowance](https://www.kela.fi/general-housing-allowance)
- From 1 April 2026, a third-country national whose residence permit application is refused loses residence-based benefits immediately, even while appealing. These benefits include general housing allowance, child benefit, medicine reimbursement and national pension. Exception: someone who applied for an extension before the old permit expired keeps entitlement until Migri decides — [InfoFinland, Kela benefits changed in spring 2026](https://infofinland.fi/w/kela-benefits-changed-in-spring-2026)
- From 1 May 2026, a new "general social security benefit" (€37.21/day, about €800/month) replaces the labour market subsidy and the basic unemployment allowance. Other non-wage income above €311/month reduces it by half of the excess. Savings and a partner's income do not count — [Kela, Changes to Kela benefits in 2026 (19 Dec 2025)](https://www.kela.fi/news/changes-to-kela-benefits-in-2026)
- Social assistance: the basic amount is €596.32 from 1 Jan 2026 and is cut to €578.43 from 1 Mar 2026. The €150 earned-income deduction was abolished on 1 Feb 2026. Private-doctor reimbursement drops from €30 to €8 from 1 Jan 2026. Housing allowance criteria are not index-adjusted for 2026 — [same Kela page](https://www.kela.fi/news/changes-to-kela-benefits-in-2026)
- Pending: the government proposes that a non-EU/EEA student's residence permit "may be withdrawn or denied if the student receives social assistance". If Parliament approves, it takes effect in October or November 2026 — [Kela news, 5 May 2026](https://www.kela.fi/news/government-proposes-to-revoke-residence-permits-of-foreign-students-who-receive-social-assistance). Whether it passed by 13 Nov 2026 was not confirmed (see Gaps). That uncertainty is exactly the kind of fact a live Exa search or Monitor should settle.

**Migri (migri.fi)**
- From 8 January 2026, the standard residence requirement for permanent residence rose from four years to six. Four years is still possible with, for example, €40,000 a year in income. Holders of a Finnish university degree have no residence-period requirement. Language skills and two years of work history are also required — [Migri, Amendments regarding permanent residence permits 2026](https://migri.fi/en/amendments-to-aliens-act-regarding-permanent-residence-permits-2026); [Migri news](https://migri.fi/en/-/aliens-act-s-provisions-on-permanent-residence-permit-amended-as-of-8-january-2026)
- Work-permit income thresholds for 2026 are on Migri's page (€1,210 / 1,090 / 1,030 net per month by region; €1,463 for part-time work or work without a collective agreement). Third-party guides quote different gross figures, which shows how stale secondary sources are — [Migri, Income requirement](https://migri.fi/en/working-in-finland/income-requirement)

**Vero (vero.fi)**
- From 2026, the home office deduction for wage income is abolished. The commuting deduction stays at up to €7,000 with a €900 threshold, per Vero. A Yrittäjät article claims a temporary €800 threshold for 2026, which conflicts with Vero — [Vero, What will change in taxation in 2026](https://www.vero.fi/en/About-us/newsroom/news/uutiset/2025/what-will-change-in-taxation-in-2026/); [Yrittäjät](https://www.yrittajat.fi/en/news/household-tax-credit-raised-sports-and-culture-benefit-increased/)
- Refund dates are individual: from July to December, shown in MyTax. The 2026 schedule includes 3 December 2026 for assessments ending in October, and refund interest is 0.5% in 2026 — [Vero, Tax refund amount and dates of payment](https://vero.fi/en/individuals/payments/refunds/tax-refund-amount-and-dates-of-payment/)

**EU261 (consilium.europa.eu, EUR-Lex, national enforcement bodies; in Finland, KKV/Traficom)**
- Council and Parliament reached a provisional agreement on 15 June 2026. The current compensation thresholds are kept: the Council's push for 4h/6h thresholds with €300/€500 did not survive conciliation — [Council press release, 15 June 2026](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2026/06/15/council-and-parliament-reach-landmark-agreement-on-stronger-eu-air-passenger-rights/) (page returned 403 on fetch; content per search summaries); [DLA Piper, 23 June 2026](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/agreement-reached-on-ec261-reform-to-strengthen-passenger-rights)
- New elements in the deal (DLA Piper): a 12-month limit to claim from the airline and 9 months to complain; airlines must contact affected passengers within four days to explain how to claim; payment or a reasoned refusal within 30 days; a codified, non-exhaustive list of extraordinary circumstances, including strikes by airport, air-navigation or ground-handling providers; self-arranged rerouting reimbursed up to 400% of the ticket; vouchers only with written consent. Expected to apply from 2027 — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/agreement-reached-on-ec261-reform-to-strengthen-passenger-rights)
- The adoption status conflicts. Some claims-firm blogs report a Parliament vote of 646 to 12 on 7 July 2026 and Council approval on 13 July. Official confirmation was not found — [Flight-delayed.com](https://www.flight-delayed.com/en/blog/2026/06/16/eu-passenger-rights-reform-2026); [g8trip](https://g8trip.com/travel-tips/eu261-compensation-rules-2026-explained)

### Inferences
- Why training data fails for candidate 1/5/10: (a) changes dated 2025-08-01, 2026-01-08, 2026-02-01, 2026-03-01, 2026-04-01 and 2026-05-01; (b) a pending law (social assistance and permit withdrawal) with a target date this very month; (c) per-case facts (nationality, permit type, degree, municipality); (d) secondary sites contradict the official ones (Migri thresholds, the Vero commuting threshold). Each of these maps to an Exa feature: date-filtered search on official domains, past page versions for before-and-after, Monitors for the pending law, and structured extraction for amounts and dates.
- For EU261, the hard live fact is not the law (stable until 2027) but the cause of a specific disruption (strike, weather, ATC), which decides whether the airline can refuse. That fact lives in news from the day of the flight, which only search can supply.

### Gaps
- Whether the student social-assistance/permit-withdrawal law passed and its exact start date (it should be checked with Exa before the demo, as the agent would).
- Exa's index coverage and past-version availability for kela.fi, migri.fi and vero.fi pages is untested. This is the biggest technical risk and must be checked in the first hour of building.
- The current residential index-clause rules on Finlex were not verified.

## 3. What "as close to done as it can" looks like, and whether a stranger or judge can test it in under a minute

### Takeaway
The candidates that finish strongest end in a concrete artifact with one approval. For internationals: a dated personal action plan, with drafted Kela/Migri/Vero messages and calendar deadlines. For flights: a claim letter citing Article 7 and the day's news on the cause. For price hikes: a termination or objection letter citing the terms the person actually agreed to. All three can be tried on a visitor's own case in under a minute, because the input is a short profile, a flight number or a pasted notice.

### Cited Findings
- Vero exposes per-person dates only in MyTax (the refund date sits on the tax decision), so an agent can explain the rules and point to the screen, but cannot read the date for the person — [Vero refunds](https://vero.fi/en/individuals/payments/refunds/tax-refund-amount-and-dates-of-payment/)
- Under the agreed EU261 reform, airlines must answer a claim within 30 days and must themselves contact passengers within four days. A drafted claim that states the cause and cites the rule fits that process — [DLA Piper](https://www.dlapiper.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/agreement-reached-on-ec261-reform-to-strengthen-passenger-rights)
- The tax-card mistake has an exact cost (60% withholding), and the fix is a walk-in visit with a signed contract. That kind of next step an agent can name precisely — [Aalto formalities, June 2026](https://www.aalto.fi/sites/default/files/2026-06/EU-citizen-Formalities-when-arriving-in-Finland_June_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The approval left to the user, per candidate: (1) internationals: "add these 4 deadlines to my calendar / send this drafted message to Kela"; (2) flights: "send the claim to the airline's form/email"; (3) price hikes: "send the termination notice"; (8) scholarships: "start application" on a ranked shortlist; (9) due diligence: "pay the deposit / walk away".
- Judge-proofing: candidate 1 handles arbitrary queries well ("I'm a Brazilian postdoc moving to Tampere in January", "does the 2026 PR rule affect someone with a Finnish bachelor's?"), because the agent searches official domains first, then everything else. Candidate 2 depends on finding the disruption's cause, which can be thin for small routes. Candidate 3 needs a real notice or contract, and judges may not have one to hand (a sample notice can be offered).

### Gaps
- Not tested: whether Kela/Migri e-services accept pre-filled data. Assume the agent drafts and links rather than submits.

## 4. What already exists and how crowded each idea will be

### Takeaway
Flight claims (AirHelp, Skycop and others) and "robot lawyer" consumer bots (DoNotPay) are established, and DoNotPay is a cautionary tale. Benefit calculators exist but are static. No product was found that tells an international in Finland what changed in their rules since a given date and what to do about it, with sources. Among Junction teams, rent and benefits checkers and generic restaurant or travel planners will be the most crowded, because the brief names them.

### Cited Findings
- AirHelp, Skycop, Flight-delayed.com, ClaimEU261 and others already publish EU261 reform explainers and run claims on commission — [AirHelp reform blog](https://www.airhelp.com/en-int/blog/what-ec261-reform-means-for-you/); [ClaimEU261](https://www.claimeu261.com/en/eu261-reform)
- In 2024 to 2025 the FTC fined DoNotPay $193,000 and barred it from claiming its AI could replace a lawyer without evidence, for an untested "robot lawyer" — [FTC](https://www.ftc.gov/node/86371); [ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/robot-lawyer-website-donotpay-settles-ftc-claims-it-couldnt-deliver-on-promises)
- Static calculators exist for Finland (for example a third-party Kela housing allowance calculator) and Vero's own household-credit calculator — [nettopalkka.fi](https://nettopalkka.fi/en/housing-allowance-calculator/); [Vero calculator](https://www.vero.fi/en/individuals/deductions/Tax-credit-for-household-expenses/calculator-for-tax-credit-for-household-expenses/)
- Previous "Best Use of Exa" winners lean towards recommendation and discovery tools. For example, TableFor at SuperAI NEXT 2026 used Exa for context-aware group recommendations, per the team's own LinkedIn post (unverified) — [LinkedIn](https://sg.linkedin.com/in/juninho-yeu)

### Inferences
- Expected crowding at Junction (judgment, not measured): very high for rent checkers, benefits finders, restaurant/date planners and trip-disruption bots, because the brief names them; high for flight compensation; medium for scholarships and funding; low for "what changed for you since you arrived" diffs, contract-terms-history checks and pre-deposit due diligence.
- The DoNotPay precedent sets the framing: "sourced information and a draft for you to send", never "legal advice" or "we guarantee".

### Gaps
- No survey of what ChatGPT or Perplexity agents return for Finnish internationals' questions was run. A quick manual comparison during the build would be strong pitch material ("ChatGPT says four years; Migri changed it to six on 8 Jan 2026").

**Top 3 in detail**

**#1 "What changed for me": rule-change briefing for internationals in Finland**
- Hook: "Tell it when you moved to Finland and why. It shows every rule that has changed for you since then, quoting each page before and after, and the three things you need to do this month."
- 30-second table demo: the visitor types "Non-EU master's student, Espoo, arrived Aug 2024, part-time job". The screen shows a timeline. 31 Jul 2025: students moved off general housing allowance to the student housing supplement. 8 Jan 2026: permanent residence now needs 6 years, or none with a Finnish degree, so the visitor's path is the degree route. 2026: home office deduction gone. 1 Apr 2026: a refused permit stops Kela benefits immediately, so apply for an extension before the old permit expires. Pending (Oct/Nov 2026): taking social assistance can cost a student their permit. Each item shows the old and new page text with links, and ends with action cards (deadline, drafted message, "add to calendar"). Approval: one tap per action.
- Judge queries and what a great answer looks like: (a) "I'm a US software engineer moving to Helsinki in January on a specialist permit, what do I do in my first 30 days?" → an ordered checklist with Migri, DVV, Vero and bank steps, the 60% tax-card trap, and sources. (b) "Did Finland's student social-assistance permit law pass?" → current status with the date and source, or "still pending as of <date>", plus the Kela page. (c) "What's the 2026 income requirement for a Finnish work permit?" → Migri's figure, with a warning that third-party guides quote different numbers. (d) "Can I still deduct my home office in 2026?" → "No for wage income from 2026" with the Vero link and the old text from a past version. (e) An arbitrary non-Finland query ("moving to Berlin as a student") should degrade gracefully: same method, sources shown, lower confidence flagged.
- Main risk: Exa's coverage and past-version availability for kela.fi, migri.fi and vero.fi, and Finnish-only pages. Mitigation: test on day one; fall back to InfoFinland (English) and university PDFs; cache the rehearsed cases.
- Caveat: frame it as "sourced information on rules that changed, not immigration or benefits advice", and link Migri, Kela and InfoFinland advice services. Never tell someone to apply or not apply for a benefit; show the risk with its source. Personal data stays in the session: don't send the visitor's nationality or permit status to anything but the search query, and phrase the query generically.

**#2 Price-increase and terms-change fighter**
- Hook: "Paste the 'we're changing your price' email. It shows what your contract said when you signed, what the law lets you do, and drafts the termination."
- Table demo: paste a sample electricity, gym or streaming notice. The agent finds the provider's terms page, pulls the version from your sign-up date, highlights the changed clause, cites the right to terminate under Finnish consumer law or the EU rules, and lists three current alternatives with prices.
